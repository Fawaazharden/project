iSpeedToLead review + 6 alternatives for motivated seller leads in 2026

By Daniel Grayson, Founder at Vocalxlabs  ·  Updated July 8, 2026  ·  8 min read

Short answer: iSpeedToLead is a legit lead marketplace, but it is a shared-marketplace model, and it is not the only way to get motivated sellers. The best alternatives depend on how hands-on you want to be. For exclusive done-for-you leads, look at Motivated Leads. For DIY data and skip tracing, BatchLeads. For a subscription feed, getsellers. And for owned, exclusive conversations at the lowest cost per contract, a done-for-you cold SMS system. Here is how they compare.

The 6 alternatives at a glance

  • Motivated Leads — exclusive PPL and custom ad management
  • getsellers — subscription pay-per-lead
  • BatchLeads — DIY data, skip tracing, and marketing tool
  • Wholesaling PPC agencies — done-for-you Google Ads
  • Direct mail services — the old standby
  • Done-for-you cold SMS (Vocalxlabs) — owned, qualified conversations

iSpeedToLead review: what it is, pricing, and reputation

iSpeedToLead is a motivated seller lead marketplace. Providers generate leads through ads, SEO, and outreach, and you buy them individually. You can preview a lead before purchasing, and there is a guarantee window that credits bad leads. It carries a 4.5 rating across hundreds of reviews and has tens of thousands of members.

Pricing is per lead: exclusive auto-delivered leads start around $100, and membership plans drop that to $29 to $39 per lead. The honest knock, echoed in reviews, is that not every lead is a winner, which is true of any marketplace. If iSpeedToLead is not the fit, here are the six real alternatives.

1. Motivated Leads: exclusive, done-for-you

An investor-run agency selling 100% exclusive leads at $300+ each, with refunds on bad leads, plus a custom option that runs Facebook, Google, and SEO in your own account starting around $2,500 per month. Good if you want exclusivity and brand-building and have the budget. Pricey per lead, and the custom route is a real monthly commitment.

2. getsellers: subscription pay-per-lead

Monthly plans at $197 to $397 deliver exclusive motivated sellers from paid social and search. Predictable if you like a set monthly cost. As with all PPL, your result rides on speed-to-lead and follow-up.

3. BatchLeads: the DIY route

Not a lead seller. BatchLeads is a tool for pulling lists, skip tracing, and marketing across 155M+ properties, at $71 to $449 per month. Powerful and cheap per record, but SMS needs a Twilio integration, dialing may need BatchDialer, and you do all the work and carry the compliance. Best for hands-on operators who want to own their data. See how to get leads with little money for the scrappy version of this.

4. Wholesaling PPC agencies: buy intent

Agencies run Google Ads to your site so high-intent sellers call you. Expect an $800 to $2,000 monthly management fee on top of ad spend, and cost per deal around $1,500 to $2,000. Strong intent, higher budget. Not a beginner's first move.

5. Direct mail: the old standby

Still works, still slow. Postcards and letters at scale, sub-1 to 2% response, and cost per deal that commonly runs $2,000+. Fine as one channel in a bigger mix, rough as your only one.

6. Done-for-you cold SMS: own the conversation

Instead of buying leads other investors also get, you text your own skip-traced market and own every conversation. Done for you, that means no systems to build and no compliance to learn, at a cost per signed contract near $100 to $300 rather than $1,500+. The tradeoff: it is outreach, so it must be run compliantly, which is exactly why having someone run it matters. More on that in is cold texting legal.

Whichever you pick, judge it on one number: cost per signed contract. A cheap lead that never closes is expensive, and a pricier channel that closes is not.
